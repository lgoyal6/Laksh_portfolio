strata

A leveled LSM-tree key-value store, written from scratch, whose durability claim is tested by killing it.

Built
Checksummed WAL, lock-free-reader skiplist memtables, prefix-compressed SSTables with Bloom filters behind a sharded block cache, leveled compaction with write backpressure, MVCC snapshots, and group commit.
Decision
Group commit: one leader appends and fsyncs the WAL for the whole waiting group, and readers only see a batch after it is applied. That is where the synchronous-commit win comes from.
Result
12,000-iteration crash matrix: 11,149 real SIGKILLs with writes torn at random offsets, 2,223,252 acknowledged writes, zero lost. CI gates 10,200 kill points per push. 50× RocksDB on synchronous commits (1,015 vs 20 ops/s).
Loses
0.65 to 0.79× RocksDB on 4-thread point reads (three global mutexes per get) and 0.43× on single-threaded scans. Both root causes are written up in the repo.

C++20 · epoll · netem · libFuzzer

taut

A reliable-UDP transport with SWIM membership, for small messages on lossy links where kernel TCP's tail latency hurts.

Built
Sliding-window ARQ with a cumulative ack plus 64-bit SACK bitmap, Jacobson/Karn adaptive RTO with a 25 ms floor, per-message reliability classes, flow control, and SWIM failure detection on a single-threaded epoll loop.
Decision
A deterministic seeded simulator, so any failure replays from one --seed, plus a libFuzzer codec harness under ASan/UBSan and a netem soak of 20/20 byte-identical 10 MB transfers at 5% loss.
Result
p99 RTT 62 to 92 ms vs 409 ms for kernel TCP at 5% loss (~6×); ~280 ms vs 3,363 ms at 20% loss (~12×).
Loses
Kernel TCP wins clean-link bulk throughput by ~27× (235 vs 8.7 Mbit/s). This is a transport for telemetry, not file transfer.

tautq

Distributed webhook delivery with no ZooKeeper and no etcd: the nodes coordinate among themselves over taut.

Built
A write-ahead log replicated by majority commit under epoch-fenced leases, with SWIM-driven failover. A partitioned owner's writes are rejected on heal, and failover inherits only majority-committed leases.
Found
Two membership-protocol gaps that taut's unit tests could not reach, including a SWIM partition-heal bug. Both are fixed upstream in taut.
Result
800 jobs/s sustained (16,000/16,000 completed), p99 107 ms at the knee. 100% completion at 0 to 20% injected UDP loss. A chaos gate runs node kill, partition, packet loss, and a corrupted log on every PR: zero lost, zero double-completed.
Loses
The knee is fsync-bound, not network-bound. The benchmark is a recorded five-node run, not a multi-host deployment.

Tollgate

A multi-tenant API gateway that lets a team share one LLM provider key safely: personal revocable keys, and the real credential injected server-side.

Built
Rate limits as atomic Redis Lua scripts (token bucket or sliding-window log, per tenant), tenant and key changes hot-reloaded from Postgres via LISTEN/NOTIFY in about a second, per-upstream circuit breakers, and OTel traces. Deploys are tag-triggered to EKS over GitHub OIDC, with no long-lived AWS credentials.
Decision
One global limit in Redis instead of per-replica counters, because a per-replica limit quietly multiplies by the replica count the moment you scale out.
Result
A 300 req/s global limit held at 9,000 admissions over 30 s across 3 replicas, where a per-replica limiter admitted 26,942 (3×). 2,000 req/s with zero errors and p99 46 ms under k6, against a mocked upstream.
Costs
Going global costs a median 0.36 ms of p50 latency over per-replica limiting, measured in-cluster on EKS over 12 paired runs.

Published result ↗

Verify-then-escalate model cascade

Is one frontier model the cheapest way to solve coding tasks? I built the harness to find out, and GMI Cloud published the result.

Built
A two-stage router, GLM 5.2 first and Opus 4.8 when the checker rejects, over 100 frozen EvalPlus tasks (50 HumanEval+, 50 MBPP+), with per-configuration token and dollar accounting.
Decision
Rank configurations by dollars per solved task instead of raw pass rate. The gain came from complementary failure modes, not from either model being uniformly better.
Result
94/100 solved vs 90 for Opus 4.8 and 89 for Fable 5, at $0.4251 vs $0.8033 (47.1% cheaper than Fable 5). A load test across 8 configurations and 500+ requests set the production concurrency cap at 16 users.
Costs
85.4% more tokens than a single model. It is a dollar-efficiency win, not a token-efficiency one.
GMI Cloud · 2026 · internal
FastAPI · PostgreSQL · Redis · React · LLM classification

Mint

An internal platform that turns event guest lists into evidence-backed sales leads a person can review.

Built
Identity resolution across Apollo and Exa evidence chains, LLM ICP tiering behind human-review gates with an audit trail, and a 16-tool conversational agent over the lead database.
Decision
Resolve identity before any paid enrichment call, dedupe company requests, and cache provider results, so the expensive APIs only see records that need them. Ingestion retries per row from idempotent checkpoints.
Result
Lead-research time cut 90% against the manual workflow. Apollo and Exa spend cut 75% against enriching every record. 25 injected ingestion failures recovered with no loss or duplication.

tickvault

Free crypto order book history is top-of-book, resampled, or silently incomplete. This records full depth and publishes its own gaps.

Built
A recorder for six venues writing to Parquet, with book reconstruction to any instant and a WebAssembly viewer. Validation is per venue (counters, CRC32, chained IDs, update-ID ranges), because each exchange offers a different proof.
Decision
Publish coverage per venue per interval, so a consumer excludes a bad window up front instead of discovering it inside a backtest.
Result
2.7M rows/s end to end. 100% clean coverage on 5 of 6 feeds. One venue went from 0% to 100% verifiable by recording it order by order: 3,999 consecutive events chained exactly.
Loses
The sixth feed is published as 0% verified rather than rounded up.
02 · Studies you can run

Claims I couldn't find a number for, so I measured them.

Each page runs the repository's own code in your browser on the committed data, so you can move the input and watch the finding hold or break.

  • decode-anatomy

    "Decode is memory-bound" is the most repeated claim in LLM inference. On an A6000 at batch 1 it is neither memory-bound nor compute-bound: 38.3% of the step is not kernel work at all, and it vanishes with batching.

    Drag the batch size ↗
  • ddp-crossover

    On two T4s over PCIe the second GPU pays on small models and stops paying on large ones, which is the opposite of the usual advice.

    Drag the model size ↗
  • noisy-rag-bench

    At 5% OCR error, retrieval recall is exactly what it was on clean documents while half the answers have already become unrecoverable. Recall is the metric that will not warn you.

    Drag the noise ↗
  • open-refusal-steering

    An open reproduction of feature-level activation steering. At the strength that reaches a 100% response rate every behavioural check passes and the model is emitting token soup. Only perplexity catches it.

    Sweep the strength ↗
  • memharness

    A memory system reads a conversation once and answers cheaply forever. The reading is an LLM pass over every turn, and it cost 12.7x what all twenty questions cost combined.

    Find the payback point ↗
  • actiongate

    Writing every extracted action item puts 18 wrong rows into the CRM out of 40. Gating at 0.65 cuts that to 2 and still auto-commits 19 of the 22 genuine ones.

    Paste a transcript ↗
  • icp-benchmark

    120 real YC companies, hand-labeled on three axes. Enriching every lead is 25% precision; a rules filter gets 93.8% and misses half the real ones. Both numbers matter and neither is published anywhere.

    Pick a filter ↗
  • promptgate

    A published production manager prompt states its reject contract four times and contradicts itself once. A parser built from the majority form reads that reject as silence, and the gate treats silence as approval.

    Break the parser ↗
  • pms-race

    A computer-use agent driving a booking screen has no transaction. Sixteen agents, one room: the legacy path books it 93 times, and the obvious fix removes that entirely while doing nothing for lost updates.

    Race 16 agents ↗
  • flightrisk

    Deposit-flight risk is U-shaped. A bank that just took a large inflow is about as likely to have a drawdown next quarter as one that just lost deposits. Every US bank, 2015 to 2026.

    Query a bank ↗
  • apparatus-comps

    A comparable-sales estimator for fire apparatus, back-tested against real sales. Half the estimates are off by more than 36%, and covering nine cases in ten takes a range nearly seven times wide.

    Price a truck ↗
